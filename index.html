Formula SAE — Year 1
Who we are

Engineering
that moves

We are Ohlone College's inaugural Formula SAE program — a team of Renegades building something most community colleges never attempt.

Formula SAE is a global collegiate design competition organized by SAE International. Teams design, build, and race a small open-wheel race car from scratch, then present it to panels of professional engineers from automotive and aerospace firms including GM, Ford, Honda, and Lockheed Martin.

Being a community college team is not a disadvantage — it is our identity. Our students are working engineers in training, many the first in their families to pursue a technical degree. We bring focus, resourcefulness, and a chip on our shoulder.

We are competing in FSAE Michigan/Lincoln — the largest engineering design competition in the world — alongside 420+ teams from 52 countries. Ohlone Renegades belong on that grid.

The car

Six systems.
One machine.

Every subsystem is student-designed. There is no kit, no shortcut, and no precedent at this school. We are building the playbook as we go.

SYS — 01

Chassis & structure

4130 chromoly steel space frame, TIG welded by students. Designed to FSAE roll-hoop and impact-attenuator rules. Every node calculated before it is cut.

SYS — 02

Suspension

Unequal-length double A-arms at all four corners. Custom machined uprights, Öhlins coilovers, and fully adjustable geometry for event-day tuning.

SYS — 03

Powertrain

Honda CBR600F4i engine through a 20mm SAE restrictor. Custom intake plenum, standalone ECU, and a stainless exhaust routed for maximum ground clearance.

SYS — 04

Brakes

Wilwood Dynalite four-piston calipers at all corners, dual-circuit pedal box with adjustable brake bias, and stainless-braided AN lines throughout.

SYS — 05

Aerodynamics

Single-element front and rear wings in fiberglass. Designed using CFD and tested against FSAE dimensional rules. Flat underbody floor for consistent ground effect.

SYS — 06

Software & data

AiM MXS dash with GPS lap timing and full sensor logging. Wheel speed at all four corners. Lap simulation in Python. ECU tuning via TunerStudio. Custom wiring harness built with Deutsch DT connectors. Every data-driven decision runs through this system.

Build schedule

The road
to competition

Phase 1
Months 1–2

Design & planning

Rules study, suspension geometry simulation, chassis design, engine selection, and procurement of long-lead items. SAE registration and faculty advisor onboarding.

Phase 2
Months 3–5

Chassis fabrication

Jig build, tube cutting, and TIG welding of the space frame. Simultaneous machining of suspension uprights, hubs, and pedal box fabrication.

Phase 3
Months 5–8

Assembly & integration

Engine install, suspension assembly, brake system, wiring harness, fuel system, and first startup. All subsystems integrated and verified before rolling the car.

Phase 4
Months 8–11

Testing & development

Shakedown, data-driven tuning, driver development, and reliability runs. Bodywork and aero fitted. Design report written. Competition tech checklist completed.

Phase 5
Months 12–14

Competition

FSAE Michigan or Lincoln. Static events: design presentation, cost report, business plan. Dynamic events: skidpad, autocross, acceleration, and endurance.

Join the team

Find your
role on the
Renegades.

We have roles for every background — engineers, fabricators, machinists, welders, electronics techs, business students, and creatives. No prior FSAE experience is required. What we need is genuine interest, honest availability, and the willingness to do real work.

Core technical roles involve direct work on the car's critical systems and require a brief calibration conversation with the system lead plus a four-week trial period. Contributor roles are open to any currently enrolled Ohlone student — you show up, you're on the team.

All team members must be currently enrolled at Ohlone College (minimum one unit). Anyone working in the shop must complete Ohlone's shop safety orientation. Drivers must hold a valid license and pass a team driver evaluation.

Suspension Lead
Geometry, dynamics & handling
Core — engineering

Defines how the car handles. Runs kinematic analysis in OptimumK, designs all four corners of the suspension in CAD, oversees machining of uprights and hubs, and sets up geometry at the track. Every handling characteristic of the car flows from this role.

What you'll do
  • Run suspension kinematics in OptimumK — camber gain, roll center, anti-dive
  • Design A-arms, uprights, stub axles, tie rods, and anti-roll bars in SolidWorks
  • Coordinate machining of uprights and hubs (CNC or manual mill)
  • Weld A-arm tubes and assemble rod ends
  • Set geometry at the car — camber, toe, ride height, corner weights
  • Tune suspension at testing and competition
Helpful background
  • SolidWorks experience required — will be doing substantial CAD work
  • Physics or dynamics coursework helpful
  • Machining experience (lathe, mill) a significant plus

How joining works

For core roles, here's the four-step process from interest to full team member.

STEP 01
Express interest

Fill out the contact form below or show up to a team meeting. Tell us which role you're interested in and a bit about your background.

STEP 02
Calibration talk

The relevant system lead has a 30-minute conversation with you — no pressure, no trick questions. We're calibrating fit, not filtering people out.

STEP 03
Trial period

You're assigned real, meaningful work for 4–6 weeks. Show up, do the work, communicate when you can't. That's all we ask.

STEP 04
Full member

After the trial, the role is yours. You're on the team, you're on the car, and you go to competition with us.
